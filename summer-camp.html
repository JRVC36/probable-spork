Summer 2026

Summer camp, by grade.

Six residential sessions for rising grades 3–12 (June 21 to July 25), plus six weeks of day camp for rising grades 1–6 (June 22 to July 31). Every session includes meals, lodging, and all activities — no add-ons, no gear to buy.

The short version

Pick a week. Pack a bag. That’s the whole process.

Campers are grouped by rising grade, not by age, so cabin groups stay level with the school year. Each session is small enough that staff know every camper’s name by the second morning.

Residential sessions run Sunday afternoon to Saturday morning. Day camp runs Monday to Friday from home, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

  • Archery, climbing, canoeing, tubing the Johns River, and hiking
  • Bible study, vespers, and campfire — faith woven through the day, not bolted on
  • One trained counselor per cabin group, plus a camp health officer on site
  • Camperships available for every session

Sibling and multi-week discounts

Second and subsequent campers from the same household receive $40 off each session, and campers attending more than one week receive $50 off each additional week. Discounts are applied when we confirm your registration.

Day camp

Home for dinner, back by morning.

Six Monday–Friday weeks in June and July for rising grades 1–6, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Tuition is $249 per week, with sibling and multi-week discounts.

Grades 1–2

The Trailblazers

Play-based learning, sensory activities, and social-emotional skills. Short hikes, creek time, crafts, and lots of unstructured outdoor play.

Grades 3–4

The Explorers

Skill-building, team challenges, and creative projects. Archery, climbing, and a weekly choice elective in art, STEM, or field games.

Grades 5–6

The Navigators

Leadership, advanced outdoor skills, and adventure. Older campers take on bigger challenges and help lead the younger groups.

A typical day

Structure, with room to get muddy.

The day has a shape so campers know what comes next — and enough open space that it still feels like summer.

  1. 8:30 — Morning rally

    Songs, the day’s goals, and a head count before anything else happens.

  2. 10:00 — Adventure block

    Outdoor sports, nature hikes, archery, or a scavenger hunt depending on the group.

  3. 12:00 — Lunch & rest

    A real meal in the dining hall, then quiet shade time for reading or journaling.

  4. 1:30 — Choice electives

    Campers pick between art, STEM, or field games. Choosing is part of the fun.

  5. 4:30 — Farewell circle

    Reflections, awards, and packing up before pickup.

Also for older campers

Counselor in Training

A leadership program for 16-year-olds who are done being campers but not done with camp.

CITs work alongside counselors for the full length of a session, learning group management, activity leadership, and how a camp day actually runs. It is unpaid, and room and board are provided.

  • Minimum age 16 at the start of the session
  • At least two years older than the campers in their group
  • One reference from a teacher, mentor, or former camp staff member
  • Full session commitment, including training and feedback meetings

Hiring for summer 2026
